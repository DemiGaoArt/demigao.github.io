// 01 — bio

Short
biography.简介

// EN

Demi Gao is a visual artist currently based in Glasgow, Scotland. Her practice moves across painting, mixed media, screenprint, and digital image-making, taking grayscale as a methodology for extending the time before meaning becomes fixed. Shaped by bodily experience, family memory, and encounters between Chinese visual culture and contemporary digital imagery, her work explores unstable identity, image degradation, and the ways matter carries time. Through recurring faces, damaged surfaces, and shifting materials, she creates spaces where different personal and cultural readings can coexist. She calls this stance soft resistance: not declaration, but a water-erodes-stone infiltration, working through rhythm, structure, and material choice to leave, slowly and persistently, a mark in the viewer's experience that has not yet been named.

// 02 — statement

Soft
resistance.艺术家陈述

// EN

My practice begins with a question that stays open: what can a work do before meaning has settled?

We live in a time that demands quick positioning: art is expected to declare, to summarise, to land. But I have found that arriving at a position too fast can turn making into a form of slogan-production. What I want, instead, is to extend the time before meaning becomes fixed. This is both a methodological discipline and a kind of courtesy toward the world.

I call this attitude grayscale. Grayscale is not ambiguity as failure, nor the absence of position; it is a respect for complexity: an acknowledgement that cognition has limits, that emotion moves, and that judgment requires the slow sedimentation of time. I do not pursue visual consistency. What stays stable is my way of understanding: across painting, mixed media, screenprint, and digital image-making, my work maintains a methodological commitment to delayed judgment, to not yet deciding.

This way of working is also rooted in personal experience. Growing up with a physical difference in my right hand made me aware very early that ideas of perfection, symmetry, and normality are often produced through the way bodies are looked at. It shaped my attraction to blurred images, broken surfaces, misalignment, and incomplete forms. For me, clarity does not necessarily equal truth, and imperfection is not something that needs to be corrected.

My cultural experience enters the work in a similarly layered way. Historical objects, family memories, fragments of Chinese visual culture, old photographs, contemporary screens, and degraded digital images can coexist within the same work. I am interested in what happens when different systems of time and meaning meet: when something inherited is placed beside something immediate, or when an old image passes through contemporary technologies of compression, scanning, and circulation. Rather than using cultural references as fixed symbols, I allow them to shift, accumulate, and be read differently in the present.

The face appears repeatedly in my work, but never as a stable marker of identity. It functions as a dynamic interface, carrying traces of emotion, duration, cultural memory, and the weight of being looked at. I am also in continuous dialogue with materials: some are used to seal time and freeze a moment inside matter; others openly admit their own disappearance, warping, or erosion. These impulses are not contradictory. They approach the same question from different sides: how matter carries time.

Image degradation, the wear of materials, the residue of repair, and the unevenness of an edge are not failures in my practice. They are the method. They keep the work open and resist its collapse into a single, complete meaning. This openness also allows different viewers to enter the work from different positions. A historical object may carry one memory for me and another association for someone else; a damaged face may be read through identity, technology, ageing, migration, memory, or bodily experience. I am interested in that gap between what I bring to the work and what another person brings to it.

For me, this is where dialogue can happen across different generations and cultural backgrounds: not because everyone reaches the same interpretation, but because the work creates a space in which different experiences can remain present at the same time.

A successful piece is therefore not defined by its level of completion, but by its ability to retain ambiguity and temporality. If an image can still provoke different paths of viewing, still resist immediate judgment, and still invite repeated re-entry, then it is alive.

Soft resistance is not retreat. It is a water-erodes-stone infiltration, working through rhythm, structure, material choice, and the slowed act of looking to leave, gradually and persistently, a mark in the viewer's experience that has not yet been named.

// 中

我的创作始于一个悬而未决的问题：在意义尘埃落定之前，一件作品能做些什么？

我们生活在一个要求快速定位的时代：艺术被期待表态、概括、迅速抵达结论。但我发现，过快地抵达某个立场，有时会让创作变成一种口号的生产。相比之下，我更想延长意义被固定之前的时间。这既是一种方法论上的自律，也是我对世界保持的一种礼貌。

我把这种态度称为“灰度”。灰度不是因为无法决定而产生的模糊，也不是立场的缺失；它是对复杂性的尊重：承认认知存在限度，情绪不断流动，而判断需要时间慢慢沉淀。我并不追求视觉风格上的一致。真正保持稳定的，是我理解世界的方式：无论是在绘画、混合媒介、丝网印刷还是数字图像创作中，我始终坚持延迟判断，保留“暂时不决定”的状态。

这种工作方式也根植于我的个人经验。因为右手的身体差异，我从很早的时候就意识到，所谓“完美”“对称”和“正常”，往往来自外部观看身体的方式。这种经验影响了我对模糊图像、破损表面、错位和不完整形式的持续兴趣。对我来说，清晰并不必然等于真实，而不完美也并不意味着某种需要被修正的缺陷。

我的文化经验也以类似的方式，分层进入作品。历史物件、家庭记忆、中国视觉文化中的碎片、旧照片、当代屏幕，以及经过降质处理的数字图像，可以同时存在于同一件作品中。我感兴趣的是，不同的时间系统和意义系统相遇时会发生什么：当某种被继承下来的事物与当下并置，或者当一张旧图像经过今天的压缩、扫描和传播技术之后，它会发生怎样的变化。相比把文化符号当作固定的象征，我更希望让它们在当代语境中继续移动、积累，并产生新的阅读方式。

面孔在我的作品中反复出现，但它从来不是一个稳定的身份标记。它更像是一个动态的界面，承载着情绪、时间、文化记忆，以及被他人观看所留下的痕迹。我也持续与材料保持对话：有些材料被我用来封存时间，把某个瞬间冻结在物质之中；另一些材料则直接承认自身会消失、变形、磨损或侵蚀。这两种方向并不矛盾。它们只是从不同的角度追问同一个问题：物质如何携带时间。

图像的降质、材料的磨损、修复留下的痕迹、边缘的不均匀，在我的实践中都不是失败。它们就是方法本身。它们让作品保持开放，抵抗作品被过早压缩成一个单一、完整的意义。也正因为这种开放性，不同的观者能够从不同的位置进入作品。一个历史物件对我而言可能承载着某种个人记忆，而对另一个人来说，它也可能激发完全不同的联想；一张受损的面孔，可以通过身份、技术、衰老、迁移、记忆或身体经验被重新理解。我感兴趣的，正是我带入作品的经验，与他人带入作品的经验之间所产生的距离。

对我来说，跨越不同代际与文化背景的对话，正是在这里发生：并不是因为所有人最终会抵达相同的理解，而是因为作品创造出一个空间，让不同的经验能够同时存在。

因此，对我来说，一件成功的作品并不取决于它的“完成度”有多高，而取决于它是否还能保留模糊性与时间性。如果一个图像仍然能够引发不同的观看路径，仍然能够抵抗快速判断，也仍然能够邀请观者一次次重新进入，那么它就是活着的。

“柔性抵抗”并不是退让。它更像一种水滴石穿式的渗透，通过节奏、结构、材料选择，以及被放慢的观看过程，缓慢而持续地在观者的经验中留下一道尚未被命名的痕迹。
